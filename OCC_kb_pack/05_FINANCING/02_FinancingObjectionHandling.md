# Financing Objection Handling — Step 8 & 9

**Purpose:** Field-ready scripts for the most common financing objections during Price Presentation (Step 8) and Closing Sequence (Step 9). Every response is tied to an approved GreenSky plan when relevant.

---

## Guardrails — Read Before Using This Document

- Use only the plan numbers, rates, and payment factors documented in `01_FinancingPlans.md`.
- Never promise approval, rate, or term — GreenSky® makes all credit decisions.
- Never volunteer the maximum discount before isolating the customer's monthly comfort zone.
- All required disclosure language is mandatory — see `03_ProhibitedFinancingClaims.md`.
- If the rep doesn't know an answer on the spot: *"I want to make sure I give you the exact number — let me confirm that and follow up."*

---

## The Step 8 Sequence (Always Follow This Order)

1. Present **List Price** first (anchor value)
2. Present a **high starting monthly payment**
3. Ask: *"How does $X per month fit into your budget?"*
4. **Briefly acknowledge** any objection → **redirect to monthly payment**
5. Once a monthly number is given → present **Monthly Promotion Offer** matched to that range
6. If still hesitating → **Add → Subtract → Sharp Angle**
7. Move to Step 9 closing sequence

**Never skip steps. Never offer discounts before isolating the monthly number.**

# The 7 Most Common Financing Objections

---

## Objection 1: "We can't afford it right now"

### What it really means
The customer is reacting emotionally to either the List Price or the high starting monthly. They have not yet been isolated on their actual monthly comfort zone.

### Goal of response
Isolate the true monthly budget → present a plan matched to that number → use Add-Subtract-Sharp Angle if still hesitant.

### Approved approach
Brief acknowledgment → redirect to monthly payment → present adjusted plan.

### Approved wording
> *"I understand it feels like a lot right now. That's why most of our homeowners finance. What monthly payment would fit comfortably in your budget so I can show you the best option we have available today?"*

> *"Got it. The list price is the full project, but the monthly payment is what really matters month-to-month. If we could get the payment into a range that works for you, would you want to move forward?"*

### Plan recommendation
- **If they need the lowest possible monthly:** Plan **9998** (180 mo, 0.96% factor) — *"On a $25,000 project, that's around $240/month."*
- **If they want a 0% APR option:** Plan **6160** (60 mo, 1.67% factor) — *"~$418/month at 0% interest."*
- **Note:** Plan 6160 carries 0% discount allowance. If they also need a price drop, lead with **Plan 6148** instead (5% discount room).

### What NOT to do
- Don't immediately drop the price or offer a discount.
- Don't assume they truly can't afford it without isolating the monthly number first.
- Don't restart product selling or re-demo.

### Return to close
> *"So, for me or anyone else to earn your business we would need to be at $___ per month or less. Is that correct?"*

---

## Objection 2: "That monthly payment is too high"

### What it really means
They accept financing exists but the starting payment exceeds their comfort zone. This is the GOOD objection — it means they've moved off total price and onto monthly.

### Goal of response
Get a specific monthly number from them → match a longer-term or lower-factor plan → re-present.

### Approved approach
Acknowledge → ask for their realistic monthly comfort zone → match the plan.

### Approved wording
> *"Fair enough. What monthly range would feel comfortable for you on a project like this?"*

> *"Got it. You're looking for something around $___ per month. So, for me or anyone else to earn your business we would need to be at $___ per month or less. Is that correct?"*

### Plan recommendation
- **Customer wants $400-500/mo on $25K:** Plan **6160** (~$418) or **2716** (~$495)
- **Customer wants $300-400/mo on $25K:** Plan **2832** (~$373) or **2740** (~$330)
- **Customer wants under $300/mo on $25K:** Plan **9998** (~$240)

### What NOT to do
- Don't randomly pick a plan — match their stated number.
- Don't promise a specific rate within a range plan (9991, 9992, 9998) — those depend on credit decision.

### Return to close
> *"At $___ per month, does this fit your budget so we can move forward today?"*

---

## Objection 3: "We don't finance / We're cash buyers"

### What it really means
Either a true cash buyer, OR a customer who associates "financing" with debt and wants to feel responsible. Confirm which.

### Goal of response
Respect the preference but still isolate a price they're comfortable with. Cash buyers still need value-justified pricing.

### Approved approach
Confirm twice → if true cash, focus on List Price discussion → still use Sharp Angle if hesitant.

### Approved wording
> *"Totally understand. A lot of our homeowners pay cash. Just so I'm clear — if the project investment lined up with what you had in mind, would you be ready to move forward today?"*

> *"And if we did move forward today, would that be a check, ACH, or card?"*

### Plan recommendation
N/A — but mention financing exists in case spouse/partner prefers it: *"Just so you know, we do have 0% APR options if that ever changes the conversation."*

### What NOT to do
- Don't push financing after they've confirmed cash twice.
- Don't assume cash buyers have unlimited budget — still build value first.
- Don't skip the disclosure that financing exists (some cash buyers convert when they see 0% APR).

### Return to close
> *"Based on the project as we've scoped it, are you comfortable moving forward today?"*

---

## Objection 4: "We want to shop other financing options"

### What it really means
Either a stall, OR a genuine comparison shopper. Either way, they're disengaging from the close.

### Goal of response
Re-emphasize the convenience and quality of on-the-spot GreenSky financing → return to monthly isolation → close today.

### Approved approach
Acknowledge → reframe convenience → present current promo → ask for the close.

### Approved wording
> *"That makes sense. The advantage of doing it through us today is the soft credit pull doesn't affect your score, you'll know your approved rate in minutes, and you lock in our current promotion. What monthly payment range were you hoping to land in?"*

> *"If we could get you approved at a payment that works for you in the next 10 minutes, would there be any reason not to move forward today?"*

### Plan recommendation
Lead with the strongest 0% APR option appropriate to project size (Plan **6148** or **6160**) — hard for outside banks to beat 0% APR.

### What NOT to do
- Don't disparage other lenders.
- Don't promise a rate before the soft pull.
- Don't let them leave the table without running the application.

### Return to close
> *"Let's run the soft pull right now. Worst case, you have a number to compare. Best case, we're done today."*

---

## Objection 5: "I need to think about it" (financing-flavored)

### What it really means
The financing didn't fully land. Either the monthly is still too high, the term feels too long, or there's a non-stated trust issue.

### Goal of response
Isolate the real concern (monthly? term? rate? trust?) → address it specifically → return to close.

### Approved approach
Use the standard "I need to think about it" objection isolation → tied to financing specifics.

### Approved wording
> *"I understand. Just so I can help — is it the monthly payment, the length of the loan, or the project itself you want to think about?"*

> *"Most people who say they need to think about it end up paying more later because today's promotion expires and project costs go up. If I could make this work with a payment that fits your budget right now, would that help you move forward today?"*

### Plan recommendation
Depends on what they isolate:
- Monthly too high → drop to longer-term plan (9998, 2832)
- Term too long → step up to shorter-term 0% plan (6136, 6148)
- Rate concern → emphasize 0% APR plans (6124-6160)

### What NOT to do
- Don't accept "I need to think about it" without isolating the specific concern.
- Don't offer a discount without first using Add-Subtract-Sharp Angle.

### Return to close
> *"So it sounds like it's worth it… it's just more than you anticipated paying today, correct?"*

---

## Objection 6: "What's the interest rate?" (asked before value is built)

### What it really means
The customer is shopping on rate alone, often before understanding the project value. Premature rate discussion kills value framing.

### Goal of response
Acknowledge briefly → defer to "after we look at the full picture" → keep building value.

### Approved approach
Don't quote rates out of context. Tie rates to plan options after the project is presented.

### Approved wording
> *"Great question. Rates depend on the term and plan that fits you best — we have everything from 0% APR options up to longer-term low-payment plans. Once we walk through the project, I'll show you exactly which plans fit your situation and what the payment looks like. Sound fair?"*

### Plan recommendation
Don't recommend yet. Wait until after Step 8 List Price presentation.

### What NOT to do
- Don't quote a specific rate before the application.
- Don't promise the lowest rate in a range plan (9991, 9992, 9998).
- Don't say "starting at 0%" without explaining the term and qualification requirements.

### Return to close
> *"Let me finish walking you through the project, and then I'll lay out the financing options side-by-side."*

---

## Objection 7: "Just give us the discount instead of financing"

### What it really means
The customer is trying to bypass financing and negotiate price. This often means they don't want a credit pull OR they think discount = better deal than monthly payment.

### Goal of response
Don't volunteer the discount. Re-anchor on monthly payment value → if they truly want cash discount, follow Add-Subtract-Sharp Angle and Step 9 close discipline.

### Approved approach
Acknowledge → reframe (financing IS the discount in disguise via 0% APR) → if they persist on cash, follow normal Step 9 sequence.

### Approved wording
> *"I hear you. Here's the thing — our 0% APR financing is essentially free money for the term of the loan. That's often a bigger savings than any discount we'd offer. Want me to show you what that looks like before we go the discount route?"*

> *"If financing isn't a fit, no problem. Let's first make sure we have the project right, then we can talk about what works."*

### Plan recommendation
- Lead with **Plan 6160** or **6148** — frame the 0% APR as the "discount."
- If they refuse financing entirely → standard Step 9 close discipline applies. Discount must be justified (Promo Home / Marketing Opportunity).

### What NOT to do
- **Don't volunteer the maximum discount.** Use Add → Subtract → Sharp Angle first.
- **Don't offer a discount above the plan's max** without manager approval.
- Don't combine discount with Plan 6160 — that plan carries 0% discount allowance.

### Return to close
> *"So it sounds like it's worth it… it's just more than you anticipated paying today, correct?"*

# Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet

| Objection | First Response | Plan to Match | Trap to Avoid |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| "Can't afford it" | Isolate monthly | 9998 (lowest) or 6160 (0%) | Discounting before isolation |
| "Payment too high" | Get their number | Match to range | Random plan pick |
| "Cash only" | Confirm twice | N/A — value sell | Pushing finance after no |
| "Shop financing" | Soft pull pitch | 6148/6160 (0% APR) | Letting them leave |
| "Think about it" | Isolate specific concern | Depends on concern | Accepting the stall |
| "What's the rate?" | Defer to plan menu | Don't quote yet | Premature rate quote |
| "Discount instead" | Reframe 0% as discount | 6160 or 6148 | Volunteering max discount |

# Hope's Coaching Triggers

Hope flags these errors during financing objection handling:

- Rep volunteers a specific discount before isolating the monthly comfort zone
- Rep quotes a rate from a range plan (9991, 9992, 9998) as if guaranteed
- Rep recommends Plan 6160 alongside a discount request (incompatible)
- Rep skips the Add-Subtract-Sharp Angle sequence and jumps to discount
- Rep accepts "I need to think about it" without isolating the real financing concern
- Rep promises approval before the soft credit pull
- Rep disparages other lenders or financing sources
- Rep restarts product selling instead of redirecting to monthly payment
- Rep uses banned language (see `03_ProhibitedFinancingClaims.md`)
