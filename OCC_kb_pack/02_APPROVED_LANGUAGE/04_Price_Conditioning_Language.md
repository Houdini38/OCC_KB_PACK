# Price Conditioning Language

## Purpose

This file controls approved language for Step 4 - Investment / Price Conditioning.

Step 4 protects premium pricing by shifting the homeowner from "what does it cost" to "what is this worth over time" before any actual price is shown.

This file supports the Sales System Master. If this file conflicts with the Sales System Master, the Sales System Master wins. If it conflicts with compliance rules, compliance wins.

---

# When To Use

Use this language after the Company Story / Challenger Method and before the Product Demonstration.

Step 4 should happen before:

- Actual project price
- Monthly payment
- Discount
- Promotion
- Financing application
- Closing sequence

---

# Psychological Principle

Value perception and contrast.

People judge price through comparison:

- Cheap vs average vs permanent
- Short-term cost vs long-term investment
- Patch today vs solve correctly
- Low price vs complete project

The rep must condition the customer to evaluate total value before seeing the price.

---

# Approved Word Track - Three Parts

## Part 1 - Investment Opening

> Mr./Mrs. [Last Name], let us talk about your investment for a moment. When you make a big purchase that you expect to last for a long time, do you usually buy the cheapest one you can find, or do you expect to pay more for a higher-quality product?

## Part 2 - Cost vs Value Frame

> I agree. Investing in your home is no different. Some homeowners focus only on the lowest price, but that can cost more later in repairs, maintenance, and replacement. Other homeowners invest in quality upfront because they want lower lifetime cost, better performance, and peace of mind. I want to compare cost versus value with you so the numbers make sense when we get to them.

## Part 3 - Transition to Demo

> Now that you see what a smart investment looks like on paper, let me show you the actual products, materials, and labor that create that value on your home.

---

# Required Customer Checkpoints

The rep should confirm the homeowner accepts the value frame before moving to product demo.

Approved checkpoints:

- "Would you rather pay less now and risk doing it again, or pay more once and have it done right?"
- "Does it make sense to look at the total picture, not just today's price?"
- "On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is long-term value and lower lifetime cost to you?"

---

# What The Rep Must Accomplish

The rep must make the homeowner understand:

1. Cheapest is not always lowest cost.
2. A complete project has more value than a patch.
3. Materials and labor both matter.
4. Cost-vs-value should be understood before price is shown.
5. The upcoming product demonstration is proof of the investment logic.

---

# What Not To Say

Do not say or imply:

- "This will pay for itself."
- "You will get all your money back."
- "This increases your home value dollar for dollar."
- "Every real estate expert agrees."
- "This guarantees resale value."
- "This will save you $____ per month" unless verified by approved documentation.

---

# Compliance Guardrail

The rep may discuss value, lower maintenance burden, curb appeal, protecting the home, and marketability.

The rep must not guarantee ROI, resale value, energy savings, or financial return.

---

# KPI Impact

Step 4 affects:

- Average Ticket
- Close Rate
- Price Resistance
- Same-Day Close Rate
- Discount Discipline

Weak Step 4 creates sticker shock in Step 8.

---

# Coaching Note

If a rep struggles with Step 8 price resistance, check whether Step 4 was skipped or weak.

A rep who does not condition price before price is usually forced to defend price after price.
