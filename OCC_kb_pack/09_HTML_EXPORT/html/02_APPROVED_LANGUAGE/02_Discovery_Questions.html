Discovery Questions

Purpose

This file controls approved discovery and inspection questions for Step 2 - Pre-Assessment / Inspection.

Discovery should help the homeowner see, understand, and own the problem before hearing the solution.

This file supports the Sales System Master. If this file conflicts with the Sales System Master, the Sales System Master wins.

Discovery Rule

Do not inspect silently.

Silent inspections create silent objections.

The rep should involve the homeowner whenever possible and use questions that create ownership, urgency, and clarity.

Required Discovery Outcomes

The rep should discover:

  1. Why the homeowner called now.
  2. What problems they have noticed.
  3. How long the problem has existed.
  4. What they have already tried.
  5. What happens if they wait.
  6. What matters most in the finished project.
  7. Who is involved in the decision.
  8. Whether timing, budget, trust, or scope concerns exist before price.

Inspection Involvement Language

I want you to walk this with me. That way when we sit down, the recommendations will make sense because you will have seen the same things I saw.

Problem Identification Questions

Use these when inspecting the home:

  • What made you start looking into this project now?
  • How long have you noticed this issue?
  • Has it gotten worse over time?
  • Have you had this repaired before?
  • What concerns you most about this area?
  • When you look at this, what do you see?
  • Is this something you expected, or is it worse than you thought?

Consequence Questions

Use these to connect the visible issue to urgency:

  • If water keeps getting behind this area, what do you think happens over time?
  • If this waits another year, do you think it gets easier or harder to fix?
  • If this had been caught five years ago, would you have handled it then?
  • Now that you know about it, when would be the best time to take care of it?

Past-Future-Present Framework

Use this framework to create urgency without pressure.

Past

If you had noticed this five years ago, would you have taken care of it then?

Future

If you wait another five years, will it be easier or cheaper to fix?

Present

Now that you know about it, when would be the best time to take care of it?

Priority Questions

Use these to understand what the homeowner values:

  • What matters most to you: appearance, durability, lower maintenance, or protecting the home?
  • What would make this project feel successful when it is done?
  • Are you trying to solve this short-term or long-term?
  • Is your bigger concern how it looks, what it may be hiding, or what it could cost later?

Decision Questions

Use these to protect decision-maker control:

  • Who else will be involved in making the final decision?
  • Is there anyone else who needs to see what we are seeing today?
  • If we find the right scope and the investment makes sense, are you comfortable making a decision today?

What Not To Do

Do not:

  • Inspect silently.
  • Ask only surface-level questions.
  • Diagnose without involving the homeowner.
  • Create fear with unsupported claims.
  • Pretend damage is confirmed if it is not visible or verified.
  • Use product claims not confirmed in product files.
  • Use warranty claims not confirmed in warranty files.

KPI Impact

Discovery affects:

  • Same-Day Close Rate
  • Close Rate
  • Objection Isolation
  • Average Ticket
  • Process Compliance

Weak discovery creates weak urgency and fake price objections later.

Coaching Note

If a customer says they need to wait, compare prices, or think about it, diagnose whether the rep created enough urgency during Step 2.
