Opening and Setting Expectations

Purpose

This file controls approved language for Step 1 - Warm-Up and appointment expectation setting.

The opening protects process control. If the rep lets the homeowner believe the appointment is only a quick quote, the homeowner will rush the rep later and price will feel unsupported.

This file supports the Sales System Master and Appointment Call Flow. If this file conflicts with the Sales System Master, the Sales System Master wins.

When To Use

Use this language at the beginning of the in-home appointment after the rep greets the homeowner.

The rep should complete this before:

  • Inspection
  • Measurement
  • Product discussion
  • Pricing
  • Financing
  • Closing

Required Outcomes

The rep must accomplish these outcomes:

  1. Greet professionally.
  2. Confirm what the office told the homeowner.
  3. Explain the appointment flow.
  4. Confirm permission to run the process.
  5. Confirm appointment length.
  6. Confirm whether the time still works.
  7. Identify decision-maker involvement.
  8. Transition into inspection with control.

Approved Opening Sequence

Part 1 - Confirm Expectations

Mr./Mrs. Customer, did the office explain what we are going to do today?

If the homeowner says, "You are here to give me an estimate," respond:

Exactly. I am going to inspect and measure your home. After that we will sit down inside and I will show you what we do and how we do it. If I can help you, I will give you exact specifications and pricing. If I cannot help, I will try to point you in the right direction. Fair enough?

Part 2 - Confirm Time

After the homeowner agrees, ask:

Did the office tell you how long this appointment usually lasts?

If the homeowner says no, respond:

It typically takes about an hour and a half, depending on how many questions you may have. Does that still work for you?

Part 3 - Transition to Inspection

Great. Let us start by walking the home together so I can see exactly what is going on and make sure the recommendations fit your house, not a generic house.

Decision-Maker Language

The rep may use natural language, but must identify who is involved in the decision.

Approved examples:

Who else will be involved in making the final decision?

Is anyone else going to weigh in on the project before you decide?

If this looks like the right project and the investment makes sense, are you comfortable making the decision today, or does someone else need to be part of that conversation?

Early Price Pressure Redirect

If the homeowner tries to rush to price, the rep should acknowledge and redirect to process.

Approved framework:

I completely understand. I will get you exact pricing today. Before I give you a number, I need to inspect the home, confirm the scope, and show you what is actually included so the number makes sense. Fair enough?

What Not To Say

Do not say:

  • "This will only take a few minutes" if that is not true.
  • "I just need to take a quick look."
  • "I can give you a ballpark now."
  • "Do you have time for the full presentation?" in a way that makes the process sound optional.
  • "We can skip all that and get to price."

KPI Impact

Opening and expectation setting affects:

  • Process Compliance
  • Close Rate
  • Same-Day Close Rate
  • Decision-Maker Control
  • Price Resistance
  • Appointment Completion Quality

Coaching Note

If a rep gets rushed later, diagnose Step 1 first.

A rushed Step 8 often starts with a weak Step 1.
