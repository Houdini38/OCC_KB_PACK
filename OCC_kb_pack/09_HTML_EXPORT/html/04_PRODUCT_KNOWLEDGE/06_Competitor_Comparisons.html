Competitor Comparisons - Approved Talk Tracks

Purpose

This document controls approved competitor-positioning language.

The goal is to differentiate SuperiorPRO without attacking named competitors, speculating, or making unsupported claims.

Universal Rules

  1. Differentiate, never attack.
  2. Do not name competitors negatively.
  3. Do not speculate about competitor solvency, crews, business practices, or quality.
  4. Use category comparisons instead of personal accusations.
  5. Loop back to the Three Ways to Do Business framework.
  6. Compare scope, process, materials, warranty documents, installation method, insurance, and follow-through.
  7. If a claim is not confirmed in approved documents, do not say it.

Missing-Information Response

Use this when asked about an unconfirmed competitor fact:

I do not have that confirmed in the knowledge base. Do not quote it on a kitchen table. Flag it to your manager so we can get an approved answer.

Category 1 - Big-Box / Nationwide Installer

How To Position

Use category language:

  • Bigger organizations may have more layers between the homeowner and the crew.
  • Large marketing operations can add overhead.
  • Customers should compare who installs the project, who manages it, what is included, and what the written warranty says.

Approved Language

The big nationwide brands often have more layers in the process: marketing, call centers, management, and subcontractor networks. What matters for you is who is actually managing the install, what is included in writing, and who is accountable after the sale.

Do Not Say

  • "They use bad crews."
  • "They are overpriced because of TV ads."
  • "They will disappear after the sale."
  • "Their warranty is worthless."

Category 2 - Cheapest Local Installer

How To Position

Use category language:

  • There is always someone cheaper.
  • Lower prices may reflect differences in scope, prep, materials, insurance, warranty, or installation standards.
  • The customer should compare what is included, not just the final number.

Approved Language

There is always someone cheaper. The question is what is included. If one quote is lower, we want to compare scope, prep, materials, warranty documents, insurance, cleanup, and installation method so you know whether you are comparing the same project.

Do Not Say

  • "They cut corners."
  • "They will do a bad job."
  • "They are not insured" unless verified.
  • "They will not be around" unless verified.

Category 3 - General Contractor / Handyman

How To Position

Use category language:

  • General contractors can be excellent for broad remodels.
  • Exterior systems often benefit from product-specific experience and manufacturer-aligned installation.
  • Customers should check whether the installer is familiar with the specific product and written requirements.

Approved Language

General contractors can be great for certain projects. With exterior systems like siding, windows, doors, paint, gutters, or stucco, the details matter. The product, prep, installation method, and written warranty terms all need to line up.

Do Not Say

  • "Handymen cannot do this."
  • "A GC will void the warranty" unless confirmed for that product and situation.
  • "They do not know what they are doing."

Category 4 - DIY / Family Member

How To Position

Use category language:

  • DIY can reduce labor cost but increases homeowner responsibility.
  • Safety, ladders, tools, product knowledge, and warranty rules matter.
  • The homeowner should consider long-term risk, not just weekend cost.

Approved Language

It is great that you have someone handy. The question is whether they specialize in this product and whether the installation will protect the home long term. With exterior work, the hidden details usually matter more than what you see from the street.

Do Not Say

  • "They cannot do it right."
  • "Your family member will mess it up."
  • "The warranty definitely will not apply" unless confirmed by the warranty file and manufacturer terms.

When Customer Names a Specific Competitor

Follow this sequence:

  1. Acknowledge respectfully.
  2. Do not attack.
  3. Ask to compare scope.
  4. Compare written details.
  5. Return to value and decision criteria.

Approved Language

I know of them. The best thing we can do is compare the details instead of the company names. Let us look at scope, materials, prep, installation method, warranty documents, cleanup, and who is responsible after the work is done.

When Customer Says SuperiorPRO Is Higher

Approved Response

Got it. Can I see what they quoted? I want to make sure we are comparing apples to apples. In this industry, two quotes can look similar until you read the fine print on materials, prep, warranty, and installation scope. Let us go line by line.

Compare These Items

  • Material grade
  • Prep work
  • Repair scope
  • Installation method
  • Crew accountability
  • Insurance coverage
  • Warranty documents
  • Cleanup and debris removal
  • Exclusions
  • Payment terms
  • Change-order rules

What Hope Coaches Against

Hope should correct reps who say:

  • "They cut corners."
  • "They are going out of business."
  • "I heard bad things about them."
  • "Their warranty is worthless."
  • "They use cheap installers."
  • "They are not insured" unless verified.
  • Any negative named competitor claim not supported by approved documentation.

KPI Impact

Strong competitor positioning supports:

  • Step 3 Company Story / Challenger Method
  • Shopping Resistance
  • Close Rate
  • Same-Day Close Rate
  • Margin Discipline

Final Rule

The strongest competitive position is to make the comparison obvious without saying anything negative about the other company.
