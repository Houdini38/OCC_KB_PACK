Financing Presentation Framework

Purpose

This document defines how financing should be presented during Step 8 - Price Presentation.

The goal is to use financing as an affordability tool while protecting margin, compliance, and the approved sales sequence.

This document does not replace the Sales System Master, Current Financing Programs, or Prohibited Financing Statements.

If this document conflicts with prohibited financing statements, prohibited financing statements win.

Core Rule

Financing supports Step 8. It does not replace Step 8.

The rep must follow the approved Step 8 sequence before moving to Step 9.

Do not move into Promo Home / Marketing Opportunity language until Step 9.

Approved Step 8 Financing Sequence

  1. Present List Price first, confidently.
  2. Present a high starting monthly payment.
  3. Ask how that monthly payment fits the homeowner's budget.
  4. If they react to the total price, acknowledge briefly and redirect to monthly.
  5. Isolate the monthly investment target.
  6. Confirm the monthly payment target.
  7. Trial-close List Price.
  8. Offer the Monthly Promotion.
  9. Match an approved plan to the stated payment range.
  10. Use Add/Subtract/Sharp Angle only if still needed.
  11. If the customer refuses financing, verify that they can pay 35% down and the balance upon completion.

Approved Step 8 Word Track

Part 1 - Anchor and Monthly Frame

The total investment for the full project comes to $List Price. You can either put 35% and pay the balance upon completion, or we can do payments at $____ per month, how does that fit into your budget?

Part 2 - Redirect and Isolate

I hear you. The full amount does look big at first. That is exactly why we look at monthly options. What monthly investment would feel comfortable for you on a project like this?

Part 3 - Payment Confirmation

Got it. You want your payments to be at $___ per month. So, for me or anyone else to earn your business we would need to be at $___ per month or less. Is that correct?

Part 4 - Trial Close List Price

If we can get your payment at $___ can we earn your business at $List Price?

Part 5 - Offer Monthly Promotion

Offer Monthly Promotion.

Part 6 - Add/Subtract/Sharp Angle

What did you like most about the product we showed you? Would you want to remove anything from the project to lower the overall investment? So it sounds like it is worth it, it is just more than you anticipated paying, correct?

Required Financing Disclosures

All Financing Presentations

Financing is provided through GreenSky and made by Synovus Bank. The lender makes all credit decisions and determines your specific rate and terms.

Required Legal Disclosure

GreenSky® program consumer loans are made by Synovus Bank, Member FDIC, NMLS #408043.

Range-Rate Plans

Use this disclosure for range-rate plans such as 9991, 9992, and 9998:

Your specific APR within this range is determined by the lender based on your application.

Deferred-Interest Plans

Use this disclosure for deferred-interest plans 2511, 2521, and 2531:

Interest is waived if the full amount is paid before the promotional period ends. If not paid in full, interest that accrued during the promo period will be charged.

Deferred Interest With Required Payments

Use this disclosure for Plan 4069:

Monthly payments are required during and after the promotional period. Interest is waived only if the full balance is paid before the promo period ends.

Payment Calculation Rule

Use the payment factor from the Current Financing Programs file.

Formula:

Project Amount x Payment Factor = Monthly Payment Estimate

Do not present estimates as guaranteed approval, guaranteed terms, or guaranteed final payment.

Matching Plans to Monthly Comfort

After the homeowner provides a monthly comfort number, match the plan to their stated range.

Do not randomly pick a plan.

Use the approved current plan table to identify the closest match.

Examples using a $25,000 project from the current financing programs file:

Customer Monthly Target Plan Direction Example Estimate
Lowest possible monthly Plan 9998, 180 months, 0.96% factor ~$240/month
Around $300-$400 Plan 2832 or 2740 depending on terms and approval ~$373 or ~$330/month on $25,000 example
Around $400-$500 Plan 6160 or Plan 2716 depending on 0% vs fixed-rate fit ~$418 or ~$495/month on $25,000 example
Wants 0% APR and can handle payment 6124, 6136, 6148, or 6160 depending on term and discount allowance Estimate based on factor

Important: these examples are estimates and are not approval promises.

Common Financing Objection Frameworks

Objection: "We cannot afford it right now"

Goal

Move from emotional reaction to monthly comfort.

Response

I hear you. The full amount does look big at first. That is exactly why we look at monthly options. What monthly investment would feel comfortable for you on a project like this?

Close-Back

If we can make the monthly investment fit, are you comfortable moving forward with the full project today?

Objection: "That monthly payment is too high"

Goal

Get a specific monthly number.

Response

Fair enough. What monthly investment would feel comfortable for you on a project like this?

Confirm

Got it. You want your payments to be at $___ per month. So, for me or anyone else to earn your business we would need to be at $___ per month or less. Is that correct?

Objection: "We do not finance / We are cash buyers"

Goal

Respect cash preference and verify ability to move forward with deposit and balance.

Response

I understand. Some homeowners prefer not to finance. If you do not want to use our financing, are you comfortable putting 35% down and paying the balance upon completion?

If They Hesitate

Return to monthly isolation without promising financing approval.

I understand. That is exactly why we look at monthly options. What monthly investment would feel comfortable for you on a project like this?

Objection: "We want to shop other financing options"

Goal

Respect the request while keeping the conversation focused on project decision and affordability.

Response

That makes sense. The key is making sure you are comparing the full project investment, terms, payment, and approval details accurately. What monthly payment range were you hoping to land in?

Guardrail

Do not disparage other lenders.

Do not promise a rate before lender approval.

Do not pressure the customer to complete an application without consent.

Objection: "I need to think about the financing"

Goal

Isolate the specific financing concern.

Response

I understand. Just so I can help, is it the monthly payment, the length of the plan, the rate, or the project itself that you want to think through?

Close-Back

If we can solve that concern, are you comfortable moving forward today?

Objection: "What is the interest rate?" before value is built

Goal

Acknowledge and defer detailed plan discussion until project scope and value are clear.

Response

Great question. Rates and terms depend on the plan and lender decision. Once we walk through the project and confirm the scope, I will show you the approved options that fit your situation and what the estimated payment looks like. Fair enough?

Guardrail

Do not quote a specific rate before the correct step or lender decision.

Objection: "Just give us the discount instead of financing"

Goal

Avoid volunteering discount and return to monthly/payment or approved close sequence.

Response

I understand. Before we talk about changing the project investment, I want to make sure we are solving the right issue. Is the concern the total price, the monthly payment, or the way the project is structured?

Guardrail

Do not call 0% APR "free money."

Do not volunteer maximum discount.

Do not combine Plan 6160 with a discount request.

What Not To Do

Do not:

  • Promise approval.
  • Promise a specific APR, payment, or term.
  • Use the phrase "free financing."
  • Misrepresent deferred interest as 0% APR.
  • Skip required disclosures.
  • Disparage outside lenders.
  • Push an application without customer consent.
  • Offer promotion before payment isolation.
  • Use Step 9 Promo Home language inside Step 8.
  • Volunteer maximum discount.
  • Recommend Plan 6160 with a discount request.

Hope Coaching Triggers

Hope should correct the rep if they:

  • Quote a range-plan rate as guaranteed.
  • Promise approval.
  • Skip disclosure language.
  • Offer a discount before payment isolation.
  • Accept "think about it" without isolating the financing concern.
  • Restart the product demo instead of redirecting to monthly comfort.
  • Use prohibited phrases from the prohibited financing file.
  • Blur Step 8 and Step 9.

KPI Impact

Financing presentation affects:

  • Financing Attachment
  • Close Rate
  • Same-Day Close Rate
  • Margin
  • Discount Discipline
  • Average Ticket

Final Rule

Financing should make the project affordable without making the rep careless.

Payment isolation first. Promotion second. Compliance always.
