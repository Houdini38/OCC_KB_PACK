Trust Reputation Concern

Customer Says

  • "How do I know I can trust you?"
  • "We have had bad experiences with contractors."
  • "I am nervous about hiring a contractor."
  • "How do I know the job will be done right?"
  • "I need to check your reviews."

What It Usually Means

The customer may not be objecting to price. They may be protecting themselves from contractor risk.

It may mean:

  1. Step 3 did not build enough trust and authority.
  2. The customer has prior contractor trauma.
  3. The homeowner does not yet understand the process.
  4. The rep has not explained scope, documentation, or warranty boundaries clearly.
  5. The homeowner needs reassurance without unsupported claims.

Where It Belongs in the 10-Step System

Primary step:

  • Step 3 - Company Story / Challenger Method

Secondary steps:

  • Step 5 - Product Demonstration
  • Step 7 - Paperwork / Scope Confirmation

Approved Response Framework

Acknowledge

I understand. A lot of homeowners are nervous because they have either had a bad contractor experience or heard stories from someone who has.

Reframe

That is exactly why process matters. The right company should be able to show you what is wrong, explain the scope clearly, document what is included, and give you written terms instead of vague promises.

Tie Back

That is why we walked the home, reviewed what we found, and built the scope around your actual project.

Isolate

What is the biggest trust concern for you: the company, the crew, the workmanship, the warranty, or the follow-through?

Close-Back Question

If we can make you feel clear on that concern, would you feel comfortable moving forward with SuperiorPRO?

KPI Impact

  • Close Rate
  • Shopping Resistance
  • Same-Day Close Rate
  • Cancellation Reduction

Do Not Say

  • "Trust me."
  • "We are the best."
  • "No one does it better than us" unless supported by approved documentation.
  • "Everything is covered."
  • "You have nothing to worry about."
  • "Other contractors will not do it right."

Compliance Guardrail

Do not invent review scores, rankings, warranty coverage, or competitor claims.

If the customer asks for a specific fact not confirmed in the knowledge base, use the missing-information response.

AI Coach Note

If trust appears late, diagnose Step 3. The rep may have given a generic company story instead of teaching the homeowner how to evaluate risk.

Practice Drill

Have the rep respond to a trust concern by explaining process and documentation without saying "trust me."
