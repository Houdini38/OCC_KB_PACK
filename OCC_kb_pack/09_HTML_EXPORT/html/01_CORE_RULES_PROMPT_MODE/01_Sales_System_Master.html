Sales System Master

Purpose

This document is the master source of truth for the 10-step sales system.

It controls:

  • The order of the sales process
  • The purpose of each step
  • The psychological principle behind each step
  • The process flow for each step
  • Approved word track structure
  • Customer checkpoints and required questions
  • KPI lesson
  • Field guardrails

This document does not override compliance, financing, warranty, product, or source-of-truth hierarchy documents.

If this document conflicts with the Conflict Priority Hierarchy, the hierarchy wins.

Core Sales System Rule

The sale is not won at the close. It is built step by step.

Each step has a job:

  1. Build trust
  2. Create urgency
  3. Differentiate the company
  4. Condition price before price
  5. Make value physical
  6. Isolate non-price objections
  7. Confirm complete scope
  8. Present price and isolate payment
  9. Close with the approved sequence
  10. Reinforce the decision and next steps

The agent must not teach reps to skip ahead unless the user specifically asks for a targeted drill.

Value Stack Estimate

Use this as a coaching estimate, not a customer-facing claim.

If perceived value must reach 100 percent before the customer buys, value is mainly built before Step 8.

System Area Value Built Training Estimate
Step 1 Trust and permission 10%
Step 2 Urgency and consequence 15%
Step 3 Company authority and differentiation 20%
Step 4 Investment logic and cost-vs-value thinking 20%
Step 5 Physical demonstration and complete project explanation 25%-30%
Step 6 Objection isolation before price 5%-10%

Important: Step 5 builds major value only when the rep explains the complete project, including materials, prep, labor, installation standards, cleanup, warranty boundaries, and what is included.

Step 1 - Warm-Up

Purpose

Personalize the interaction, set control of the appointment, and earn permission to run the full process.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Reciprocity and relationship building. The homeowner becomes more cooperative when the rep is genuinely respectful, organized, and helpful before asking for commitment.

Process Flow

  1. Greet professionally and thank the homeowner for inviting SuperiorPRO into the home.
  2. Confirm who is present and who is involved in the decision.
  3. Set the agenda: inspect, measure, sit down, review the process, then provide exact specifications and pricing.
  4. Confirm appointment time so the rep does not get rushed later.
  5. Transition into the inspection with control and confidence.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Greeting and Agenda

Mr./Mrs. Customer, did the office explain what we are going to do today?

If they say, "Give me an estimate," respond:

Exactly. I am going to inspect and measure your home. After that we will sit down inside and I will show you what we do and how we do it. If I can help you, I will give you exact specifications and pricing. If I cannot help, I will try to point you in the right direction. Fair enough?

Part 2 - Time Confirmation

After they say fair enough:

Did the office tell you how long this appointment usually lasts?

Customer: "No."

Rep:

It typically takes about an hour and a half, depending on how many questions you may have. Does that still work for you?

Part 3 - Inspection Transition

Great. Let us start by walking the home together so I can see exactly what is going on and make sure the recommendations fit your house, not a generic house.

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Fair enough?
  • Does that still work for you?
  • Who else will be involved in making the final decision?

KPI Lesson

Close Rate and Process Compliance. If the rep loses control here, every later step gets rushed or challenged.

Field Guardrail

Do not start diagnosing or selling before the homeowner has agreed to the process.

Step 2 - Pre-Assessment / Inspection

Purpose

Create urgency by helping the homeowner see and own the problem before hearing a solution.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Commitment and consistency, supported by loss aversion. Once the homeowner agrees the issue is real and likely to worsen, waiting becomes harder to defend.

Process Flow

  1. Walk the property with the homeowner whenever possible.
  2. Take photos of problem areas: wide-angle context and close-up detail.
  3. Use the homeowner's own words when documenting issues.
  4. Point out cause, consequence, and timing.
  5. Use Past-Future-Present to convert damage into urgency.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Bring Them Into the Inspection

I want you to walk this with me. That way when we sit down, the recommendations will make sense because you will have seen the same things I saw.

Part 2 - Identify the Issue on Their Home

Here is what I am seeing on your home: [specific issue]. The concern is not just how it looks today. The concern is what happens if water keeps getting behind this area.

Part 3 - Past-Future-Present

If you had noticed this five years ago, would you have taken care of it then? If you wait another five years, will it be easier or cheaper to fix? Now that you know about it, when would be the best time to take care of it?

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Do you see what I am pointing out here?
  • Would you agree this is something that needs to be addressed?
  • When would be the best time to take care of it?

KPI Lesson

Same-Day Close Rate. Urgency built here reduces "we need to wait" later.

Field Guardrail

Do not inspect silently. Silent inspections create silent objections.

Step 3 - Company Story / Challenger Method

Purpose

Teach the homeowner how to think about the project, tailor the risk to their home, and take control of the decision framework.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Authority and differentiation. The homeowner should see SuperiorPRO as the safer, smarter path compared with DIY or a partial contractor solution.

Process Flow

  1. Transition from the inspection into the Three Ways to Do Business framework.
  2. Teach why partial jobs and patch fixes create long-term risk.
  3. Tailor every point to photos and issues found on their home.
  4. Take control by explaining SuperiorPRO only recommends the right long-term scope.
  5. Secure agreement that the homeowner wants the job done once and done right.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Teach: Do It Yourself

Now that we have walked the house and seen exactly what is going on, let me show you the three ways you can get this project done so you can decide which path is truly best for your home. First option: do it yourself. Have you thought about handling this on your own? If you did, you would be responsible for the material choice, installation, warranty paperwork, and any failure later. That puts all the risk on you.

Part 2 - Tailor: Hire a Contractor

Second option: hire a local contractor. Most homeowners start here. But on your home, the danger is a partial fix. A contractor may agree to paint and replace a few bad boards because that is what was requested, but if the underlying issue is bigger, the problem can come back. Is it safe to say you want the job done once and done right?

Part 3 - Take Control: Choose SuperiorPRO

The third option is SuperiorPRO. We only recommend doing the job the way the manufacturer designed it and the way the home actually needs it. If the right answer is full replacement to spec, we will say that. If a patch is not the right long-term fix, we will tell you. The point is no finger-pointing and no guesswork. We solve the problem the way it should be solved.

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Is it safe to say you want the job done once and done right?
  • Do we seem like the type of company you would feel comfortable doing business with?
  • If SuperiorPRO can meet the quality you are looking for and only recommend the right way to do the job, would you consider us your number-one choice?

KPI Lesson

Close Rate and Shopping Resistance. This step reduces "we need three prices" by reframing the decision from price shopping to risk selection.

Field Guardrail

Differentiate without attacking. Do not make broad warranty promises like "we stand behind everything 100 percent" unless it is tied to written warranty terms.

Step 4 - Investment / Price Conditioning

Purpose

Shift the homeowner from "what does it cost" to "what is this worth over time" before any price is shown.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Value perception and contrast. People judge price through comparison: cheap vs average vs permanent, short-term expense vs long-term investment.

Process Flow

  1. Transition smoothly from the Company Story.
  2. Use the word "investment" instead of price or cost.
  3. Show the Cost vs Value framework or report.
  4. Explain lifetime cost, maintenance avoided, durability, warranty boundaries, and resale considerations.
  5. Use the cheap-average-permanent comparison to show why the cheapest option can be the most expensive over time.
  6. Get verbal agreement before moving to the product demo.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Investment Opening

Mr./Mrs. [Last Name], let us talk about your investment for a moment. When you make a big purchase that you expect to last for a long time, do you usually buy the cheapest one you can find, or do you expect to pay more for a higher-quality product?

Part 2 - Cost vs Value Frame

I agree. Investing in your home is no different. Some homeowners focus only on the lowest price, but that can cost more later in repairs, maintenance, and replacement. Other homeowners invest in quality upfront because they want lower lifetime cost, better performance, and peace of mind. I want to compare cost versus value with you so the numbers make sense when we get to them.

Part 3 - Transition to Demo

Now that you see what a smart investment looks like on paper, let me show you the actual products, materials, and labor that create that value on your home.

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Would you rather pay less now and risk doing it again, or pay more once and have it done right?
  • Does it make sense to look at the total picture, not just today's price?
  • On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is long-term value and lower lifetime cost to you?

KPI Lesson

Average Ticket and Close Rate. Step 4 protects premium pricing before the rep ever shows price.

Field Guardrail

Never reveal actual pricing or monthly payments in Step 4. If they ask early, say: "I will give you exact numbers in just a few minutes. First I want to make sure you see exactly what you are investing in so the numbers make sense."

Step 5 - Product Demonstration

Purpose

Turn spoken value into physical proof. The homeowner should see, touch, and understand why the complete project is worth the investment.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Value perception and contrast. The demo lets the customer compare weak vs strong materials, incomplete vs complete scope, and cheap labor vs professional installation.

Process Flow

  1. Demonstrate the materials with hands-on comparisons.
  2. Explain what is included in a complete project: prep, removal, repair, installation, materials, labor, protection, cleanup, and warranty boundaries.
  3. Connect every feature to a homeowner benefit.
  4. Use their Step 2 photos to make the demo specific to their home.
  5. Confirm they understand what makes the complete project different from a patch job.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Demo the Problem

Remember the area we photographed outside? This is why that matters. The visible issue is only part of the story. The bigger issue is what is happening behind or around it if it is not corrected properly.

Part 2 - Demo the Product and Materials

Here is the material we use, here is how it is installed, and here is why it performs better than the lower-grade option. I want you to feel the difference, not just hear me talk about it.

Part 3 - Explain the Complete Project

The value is not just the product. It is the full system: removal, prep, repairs, proper installation, materials, skilled labor, cleanup, documentation, and written warranty terms. That is what you are investing in.

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Can you see why this is different from a patch-and-paint approach?
  • Does the complete project make sense based on what we saw on your home?
  • What part of this matters most to you: appearance, durability, lower maintenance, or protecting the home?

KPI Lesson

Average Ticket and Same-Day Close. Internal training estimate: the physical demonstration should build roughly 25 percent to 30 percent of the customer's total perceived value before price.

Field Guardrail

Do not show samples like a science fair. Explain the materials and the labor involved in the complete project. Great value is built when the customer understands everything included.

Step 6 - Pre-Close

Purpose

Clear everything except price before paperwork and price presentation.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Commitment, consistency, and objection isolation. The homeowner confirms the project is needed, the company is acceptable, and price is the only remaining variable.

Process Flow

  1. Confirm custom-order logic.
  2. Confirm the project is a when decision, not an if decision.
  3. Ask directly whether anything besides investment would prevent them from doing business.
  4. Handle any non-price objection before moving forward.
  5. Do not assume silence means agreement.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Custom Order Logic

Because every home is unique, we do not order anything until you are ready to move forward. You can understand that, right?

Part 2 - If vs When

As we discussed, this is not really a matter of if this project is going to get done. It is a matter of when. Correct?

Part 3 - Isolate Non-Price Objections

After everything I have shown you today, is there any other reason, other than the investment, that would keep you from doing business with me?

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • You can understand that, right?
  • Correct?
  • Any other reason, other than the investment?

KPI Lesson

Same-Day Close Rate. This step prevents fake price objections that are actually trust, scope, product, or decision-maker objections.

Field Guardrail

If they give a non-price objection, stop and solve it. Do not push into price with an unresolved concern.

Step 7 - Paperwork / Scope Confirmation

Purpose

Make the scope accurate, reduce production risk, and make the project feel real before pricing.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Specificity and risk reduction. Clear scope lowers uncertainty and increases confidence.

Process Flow

  1. Complete spec sheets accurately and legibly.
  2. Explain each major scope item and inclusion.
  3. Confirm measurements, options, colors, adders, and exclusions.
  4. Use paperwork to reinforce professionalism, not to pressure.
  5. Get homeowner confirmation before moving into price.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Frame the Paperwork

This paperwork is just to make sure the scope of work is 100 percent accurate, so when you are ready, everything is correct.

Part 2 - Explain the Complete Scope

I am going to list the materials, labor, prep, repairs, installation details, cleanup, and any options we discussed so there is no confusion later.

Part 3 - Confirm Accuracy

Before I price this, look this over with me. Does this match the project you want completed?

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Does this match what you want completed?
  • Did I miss anything important?
  • Are these the correct options and details?

KPI Lesson

Process Compliance, Production Accuracy, and Cancellation Reduction. Clean paperwork protects the sale after the rep leaves.

Field Guardrail

Do not treat paperwork as admin. It is the bridge between value and investment.

Step 8 - Price Presentation

Purpose

Present price without losing control, minimize sticker shock, isolate monthly comfort, and move only when the customer gives a number or rejects financing clearly.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Anchoring and mental accounting. The list price anchors value. The monthly payment reframes a large investment into a manageable budget decision.

Process Flow

  1. Present List Price first, confidently.
  2. Present a high starting monthly payment.
  3. Ask how that monthly payment fits their budget.
  4. If they react to total price, acknowledge briefly and redirect to monthly.
  5. Do not offer any promotion until a monthly number is isolated or they clearly refuse financing.
  6. After the Monthly Promotion Offer, use Add/Subtract/Sharp Angle only if still needed.
  7. If customer says they do not want to use our financing, verify that they can pay the 35% deposit and move forward.

Word Track - Approved Sequence

Part 1 - Anchor and Monthly Frame

The total investment for the full project comes to $List Price. You can either put 35% and pay the balance upon completion, or we can do payments at $____ per month, how does that fit into your budget?

Part 2 - Redirect and Isolate

I hear you. The full amount does look big at first. That is exactly why we look at monthly options. What monthly investment would feel comfortable for you on a project like this?

Part 3 - Payment Confirmation

Got it. You want your payments to be at $___ per month. So, for me or anyone else to earn your business we would need to be at $___ per month or less. Is that correct?

Part 4 - Trial Close List Price

If we can get your payment at $___ can we earn your business at $List Price?

Part 5 - Offer Monthly Promotion

Offer Monthly Promotion.

Part 6 - Add/Subtract/Sharp Angle

What did you like most about the product we showed you? Would you want to remove anything from the project to lower the overall investment? So it sounds like it is worth it, it is just more than you anticipated paying, correct?

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Required question: "How does $____ per month fit into your budget?"
  • Required isolation: "What monthly investment would feel comfortable for you?"
  • Credit checkpoint: "One being the worst and Ten the best, where would you put your credit?"
  • Required progression: do not move to Step 9 until the customer agrees it is worth it but more than expected.

KPI Lesson

Financing Attachment, Close Rate, and Margin. Step 8 prevents panic discounting and keeps the conversation on affordability.

Field Guardrail

Never offer a discount before isolating monthly payment. Never defend the total for more than a few seconds. Never use Add/Subtract/Sharp Angle before presenting the Monthly Promotion Offer.

Step 9 - Closing Sequence

Purpose

Convert the customer's "worth it" agreement into a same-day decision using the approved close sequence.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Reactance reduction, scarcity and justification, loss aversion, and cognitive dissonance reduction. The customer feels less pressured, sees the offer as justified, and has a clean path to act on what they already agreed is worth it.

Process Flow

  1. Begin immediately after the Sharp Angle agreement.
  2. Physically disarm: sit back, put the pen down, begin stacking papers, relax posture.
  3. Ask the timeline question.
  4. Deliver the WOW line no matter what timeline they give.
  5. Introduce the Promo Home / Marketing Opportunity as a justified First Drop.
  6. Present the adjusted offer.
  7. Ask for the sale directly.
  8. If they still hesitate, isolate the final objection and do not stack unapproved discounts.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Disarm and Timeline

[Physically disarm.] How soon do you think you would be able to get back with me?

Part 2 - WOW Line and Justified First Drop

Wow, you must be pretty motivated to get this project done. I tell you what, It is my job to keep orders flowing into the warehouse and while I’d love to get your business next week the truth is, I need to earn it today. I do have something that might help both of us out. All I ask is that you keep an open mind, FAIR ENOUGH???. Great, our marketing dept. has set aside some marketing money instead of spending it on TV and radio. We use it as a special incentive for homeowners who are ready to move forward. Let me show you what that would look like.

Part 3 - Direct Close and Final Objection Control

Based on this adjusted offer, are you comfortable moving forward with the project today?

If they hesitate:

I understand. What specifically is holding you back at this point: the project, the company, the timing, or the investment?

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Required move: physical disarm before the timeline question.
  • Required line: "Wow, you must be pretty motivated to get this project done."
  • Required ask: "Based on this adjusted offer, are you comfortable moving forward with the project today?"

KPI Lesson

Same-Day Close Rate and Margin. Step 9 gives the rep a controlled path to close without appearing desperate or throwing money at the objection.

Field Guardrail

The First Drop must be justified. Never frame it as "let me see what I can do," "my manager might approve," or "you twisted my arm."

Step 10 - Warm Down

Purpose

Secure the sale emotionally, reduce buyer's remorse, confirm next steps, and open the door for reviews and referrals.

Psychological Principle and Purpose

Post-decision reinforcement and consistency. After a customer says yes, they need to feel calm, confident, and validated so the decision sticks.

Process Flow

  1. Slow down after the signature.
  2. Spend a few minutes in normal conversation instead of rushing out.
  3. Confirm contact information, installation expectations, and next steps.
  4. Set expectation for communication and follow-up.
  5. Ask for review or referral at the right time.

Word Track - Three Parts

Part 1 - Reinforce the Decision

You made a smart decision. Based on what we saw today and what you told me mattered most, this is the right scope for your home.

Part 2 - Confirm Next Steps

Here is what happens next: we will confirm the paperwork, communicate scheduling, and keep you updated as the project moves forward.

Part 3 - Relationship Close

Before I head out, I want to make sure you feel clear on everything. What questions do you have about what happens next?

Customer Checkpoints / Required Questions

  • Do you feel clear on the next steps?
  • Is this the best number and email for project updates?
  • Who else do you know who may be thinking about a project like this?

KPI Lesson

Cancellation Rate, Reviews, Referrals, and Customer Experience. A strong warm down keeps the deal from cooling off after the rep leaves.

Field Guardrail

Do not sprint out after the signature. A rushed exit can create buyer's remorse.

Agent Coaching Rules For This Master

When teaching the system, the agent should:

  1. Name the step.
  2. Explain the step's purpose.
  3. Explain the psychological principle.
  4. Give the process flow.
  5. Demonstrate approved language.
  6. Identify customer checkpoints.
  7. Tie the step to a KPI.
  8. Give one practice drill.

When a rep skips a step, the agent should name the missed step and explain the KPI impact.

When a rep blends Step 8 and Step 9, the agent must correct it immediately.

When a rep asks for alternate language in Step 8 or Step 9, the agent must preserve the approved sequence and avoid language drift.

Final Rule

The 10-step sales system is a sequence, not a menu. The agent may train individual steps, but it must not teach the rep to skip value-building steps and jump straight to price or close.
