# Common Sales Mistakes

* **Document Type**: Diagnostic
* **Status**: Complete

* **Applies To**: All reps
* **Mode Relevance**: Coach

## Purpose

List the most common rep mistakes Hope should diagnose quickly.

## Common Sales Mistakes by Step

### Step 1: Warm-Up

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Rushing or completely skipping personalization – jumping straight into business talk|Step 1: Warm-Up|Rep is overly transaction-focused or nervous about small talk|Run 10+ daily warm-up role-plays focused on personal questions (family, pets, neighborhood, how long lived here). Record and critique delivery.|Immediate resistance and low trust; appointment flow drops 30–40%|
|Failing to deliver the exact Initial Greeting Statement or properly set expectations|Step 1: Warm-Up|Script not memorized; rep doesn’t understand its power to control the appointment|Mandatory word-for-word memorization drills + “just give me a price” objection role-plays until flawless.|High early objections, shortened appointments, close rate <25%|
|Failing to confirm how much time the customer has set aside|Step 1: Warm-Up|Rep assumes the call center informed the customer about time|Drill full greeting sequence including “Did the office tell you how long this would take?” and require customer affirmation in every role-play.|Misaligned expectations; customer runs out of time, unable to build value.|

### Step 2: Pre-Assessment

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Superficial or rushed walk-around with no detailed notes, photos, or homeowner involvement|Step 2: Pre-Assessment|Rep wants to get to the table quickly or lacks thoroughness|Simulate real property issues; require full notes/photos and verbal walkthrough with customer in every role-play.|No emotional investment created; urgency is weak, deal size shrinks|
|Skipping or poorly delivering the Past-Future-Present tie-down questions|Step 2: Pre-Assessment|Rep uncomfortable with tough questions or hasn’t internalized the framework|Drill the exact 3 PFP questions (“If this happened five years ago…”, “Will it get better or worse?”, “Best time is now?”) until automatic.|“We’ll think about it” stalls dominate; close rate cut in half|
|Failing to tie discovered issues back to the customer’s original reason for the appointment|Step 2: Pre-Assessment|Rep inspects but doesn’t connect problems to homeowner’s pain|Role-play “What’s the main reason…” opener + linking every issue back to their stated goal.|Customer remains emotionally detached; low urgency and lower conversion|

### Step 3: Company Story

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Weak or incomplete “3 Ways to Do Business” presentation (skipping sections or rushing)|Step 3: Company Story|Rep doesn’t know the full script or fears direct comparison|Full scripted role-play of DIY → Local Contractor → SuperiorPRO with every tie-down. Critique energy and conviction.|Customer believes “all companies are the same”; shopping around increases 25%+|
|Failing to use interactive tie-downs or qualifying questions on the 4-Square page|Step 3: Company Story|Rep delivers monologue instead of conversation|Practice interactive 4-Square + tie-downs (“Is it safe to say…?”, “Would you consider us #1…?”) in every drill.|Poor differentiation; weak confidence in SuperiorPRO; higher “need estimates” objections|
|Not highlighting exclusive manufacturer partnerships and single-point accountability|Step 3: Company Story|Rep glosses over what truly makes SuperiorPRO different|Role-play the full Company Story section with emphasis on partnerships (James Hardie, Provia, etc.) and control of manufacturing/retail/installation.|Low perceived uniqueness; close rate drops 20–30%|

### Step 4: Investment (Financial Discussion)

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Jumping straight into price without proper Cost vs Value / ROI conditioning|Step 4: Investment|Rep is price-focused rather than value-focused|Coach exact Cost vs Value framing and ROI examples before showing any numbers. Use multiple customer profiles in drills.|Severe sticker shock; “too expensive” objections dominate|
|Failing to shift the customer’s mindset from “cost” to long-term “investment”|Step 4: Investment|Rep doesn’t fully believe in or can’t articulate the value story|Repetition drills comparing cheap vs premium lifetime costs and financing benefits.|Price resistance stays high; average sale price and financing take-rate drop|
|Not preparing the customer for the investment range before revealing numbers|Step 4: Investment|Rep skips price conditioning entirely|Role-play full investment discussion with clear “some invest in quality upfront…” language every time.|Massive price objections; lower close probability|

### Step 5: Product Demo

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Weak hands-on demo – talking about products instead of letting customer touch/feel/test|Step 5: Product Demo|Rep relies on slides or verbal explanation only|Hands-on drills with physical samples. Must demonstrate durability and get sensory engagement.|Perceived parity with cheaper options; “shop around” objections rise|
|No clear feature-to-benefit linking or competitive comparisons|Step 5: Product Demo|Rep lacks deep product knowledge|Role-play feature → benefit → tie-down sequence. Drill “Why is this better than X?” objections.|Low desire and emotional connection; weak later-stage closes|
|Failing to tie product benefits directly to the specific problems found in Pre-Assessment|Step 5: Product Demo|Rep does generic demo instead of personalized one|Require reps to reference inspection photos/issues during every product demo in role-play.|Customer doesn’t see personal relevance; conversion suffers|

### Step 6: Pre-Close

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Skipping or incorrectly delivering the exact 3-point Pre-Close Statement|Step 6: Pre-Close|Rep is nervous about price reveal or hasn’t memorized the script|Word-for-word memorization + role-play until 100% consistent on the full statement.|Non-price objections explode at pricing; many deals die|
|Leaving non-price objections unaddressed before moving to price|Step 6: Pre-Close|Rep avoids conflict or doesn’t isolate objections properly|Isolate-and-clear drills for every possible non-price objection. Score on thoroughness.|Objection avalanche later; dramatically lower conversion|
|Failing to confirm “it’s not a matter of IF but WHEN” during pre-close|Step 6: Pre-Close|Rep rushes or forgets key tie-down language|Mandatory inclusion of the full Pre-Close script in every role-play.|Customer stays in “maybe later” mindset; same-day close rate drops|

### Step 7: Paperwork

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Rushing through spec sheets without full customer confirmation on every line item|Step 7: Paperwork|Rep is eager to reach pricing|Slow-motion role-play of complete spec sheet walkthrough with verbal confirmation on each item.|Scope misunderstandings, change orders, and regret later|
|Inaccurate or incomplete paperwork leading to execution problems|Step 7: Paperwork|Rep lacks attention to detail or proper training on forms|Checklist drills + double-review process. Emphasize “do not rush” principle.|Project complications, lower review scores, lost referrals|
|Not explaining the purpose of the paperwork clearly to reduce signing anxiety|Step 7: Paperwork|Rep treats paperwork as just administrative|Role-play clear explanation: “This is just to ensure accuracy in our scope of work…” every time.|Customer hesitation or refusal to sign; stalls before pricing|

### Step 8: Price Presentation

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|No List Price anchor – jumping straight to discounted or monthly payment|Step 8: Price Presentation|Rep uncomfortable presenting high numbers|Strict sequence drills: List Price → Starting Payment → Monthly Promo Offer. Record every time.|Sticker shock; perceived low value; collapsed deal size|
|Skipping the Add-Subtract-Sharp Angle technique on payment objections|Step 8: Price Presentation|Rep doesn’t trust or remember the full technique|Daily Add-Subtract-Sharp Angle drills including “act defeated” body language.|No value rebuilding; high fallout at pricing stage|
|Failing to isolate the monthly payment concern before offering solutions|Step 8: Price Presentation|Rep tries to overcome price objection too broadly|Role-play payment isolation + financing adjustment based on customer feedback.|Customer feels overwhelmed; lost same-day closes|

### Step 9: Closing Sequence

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Accepting “I need to think about it” without strong urgency language or incentives|Step 9: Closing Sequence|Rep lacks closing confidence or tools|Daily role-plays of every major objection using Promo Home Opportunity, Feel-Felt-Found, and pie close.|Think-it-over rate >50%; massive drop in same-day closes|
|Offering price drops without proper justification or scarcity framing|Step 9: Closing Sequence|Rep gives discounts too easily|Drill First Drop Justification tied to limited-time promo only. Enforce “reasonable \& justified” rule.|Margin erosion and expectation of bigger discounts|
|Not using Feel-Felt-Found or third-party stories when handling final objections|Step 9: Closing Sequence|Rep relies only on logic instead of emotion + social proof|Mandatory inclusion of Feel-Felt-Found and success stories in every closing role-play.|Emotional resistance remains; lower overall close rate|

### Step 10: Warm Down

|Rep Mistake|Where it shows up|Root cause|How Hope should correct it|Likely KPI impact|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|Rushing out the door immediately after getting the signature|Step 10: Warm Down|Rep thinks the job is “done” once signed|Full warm-down role-plays focusing on personal conversation and rapport building.|Lower review scores; fewer referrals|
|No review request or effort to solidify long-term relationship|Step 10: Warm Down|Rep uncomfortable asking for reviews|Scripted drills ending with review ask + personal connection. Track referral KPIs.|Missed referral and repeat business opportunities|
|Failing to leave proper contact info and next-step clarity|Step 10: Warm Down|Rep ends transactionally instead of relationally|Role-play full handover of documents, contact info, and clear timeline expectations.|Customer confusion post-sale; reduced satisfaction and referrals|
