# Appointment Call Flow

## Purpose

This document defines the appointment expectation flow that the AI coach should teach, roleplay, and diagnose.

The goal is to protect Step 1 Warm-Up and prevent the rep from being rushed into price before trust, urgency, value, scope, and payment control have been built.

This document supports the Sales System Master. If this document conflicts with the Sales System Master, the Sales System Master wins.

---

# Core Rule

A rushed appointment creates a weak close.

The rep must set expectations early so the homeowner understands:

- What will happen during the appointment
- Why the inspection matters
- Why both decision-makers should be involved
- How long the appointment usually takes
- That price comes after inspection, education, scope, and value

---

# Appointment Expectation Goals

The appointment flow should accomplish these outcomes:

1. Confirm the homeowner knows who the rep is and why they are there.
2. Confirm the homeowner understands the appointment structure.
3. Confirm the homeowner has enough time for the appointment.
4. Confirm the decision-making situation.
5. Prevent the homeowner from forcing a premature price conversation.
6. Transition into inspection with control and professionalism.

---

# Step 1 In-Home Appointment Opening

Use this during the in-home appointment after greeting the homeowner.

## Required Sequence

1. Ask whether the office explained what would happen.
2. Explain the appointment flow.
3. Ask for agreement.
4. Confirm appointment length.
5. Confirm whether that still works.
6. Transition into inspection.

## Approved Opening Language

> Mr./Mrs. Customer, did the office explain what we are going to do today?

If the homeowner says something like, "You are here to give me an estimate," respond:

> Exactly. I am going to inspect and measure your home. After that we will sit down inside and I will show you what we do and how we do it. If I can help you, I will give you exact specifications and pricing. If I cannot help, I will try to point you in the right direction. Fair enough?

After the homeowner says fair enough, ask:

> Did the office tell you how long this appointment usually lasts?

If the homeowner says no, respond:

> It typically takes about an hour and a half, depending on how many questions you may have. Does that still work for you?

Then transition:

> Great. Let us start by walking the home together so I can see exactly what is going on and make sure the recommendations fit your house, not a generic house.

# Decision-Maker Confirmation

## Purpose

The rep must understand who is involved in the decision before the appointment moves too far forward.

A decision-maker issue that is ignored in Step 1 usually becomes a stall in Step 8 or Step 9.

## Required Outcome

The rep should know whether:

- All decision-makers are present
- Another person needs to be involved later
- The homeowner can make the decision alone
- The appointment is at risk of becoming a one-legger

## Example Questions

The rep may use natural language, but must accomplish the outcome.

Examples:

> Who else will be involved in making the final decision?

> Is anyone else going to weigh in on the project before you decide?

> If this looks like the right project and the investment makes sense, are you comfortable making the decision today, or does someone else need to be part of that conversation?

---

# When the Homeowner Wants to Rush to Price

## Purpose

The rep must not allow the homeowner to skip straight to price.

Early price without inspection, value, scope, and payment control creates sticker shock and weakens close rate.

## Approved Redirect Framework

The wording can be natural, but the rep must communicate:

1. Price depends on inspection and scope.
2. The rep will provide exact pricing.
3. The rep needs to inspect and explain the project first.
4. The homeowner will understand the number better after the process.

Example:

> I completely understand. I will get you exact pricing today. Before I give you a number, I need to inspect the home, confirm the scope, and show you what is actually included so the number makes sense. Fair enough?

## What Not To Do

The rep must not:

- Give a rough price before inspection
- Apologize for needing to run the process
- Say "this will only take a few minutes" if that is not true
- Skip value-building steps to satisfy impatience
- Defend price before value exists

# Appointment Timing Rule

## Standard Expectation

The appointment typically takes about an hour and a half, depending on homeowner questions and project complexity.

## Coaching Rule

If a rep fails to confirm time, Hope should correct it because time pressure later damages the close.

Correction example:

```text
You missed the appointment-length confirmation in Step 1.
Why it matters: If the customer thinks this is a quick estimate, they will resist the full process and rush you at price.
Corrected move: Ask, "Did the office tell you how long this appointment usually lasts?" Then explain the appointment typically takes about an hour and a half depending on questions.
```

---

# Inspection Transition Rule

The rep should transition into inspection only after the homeowner agrees to the appointment flow and timing.

The inspection should not feel like the rep disappearing outside to collect measurements.

The homeowner should be included whenever possible.

Approved transition:

> Great. Let us start by walking the home together so I can see exactly what is going on and make sure the recommendations fit your house, not a generic house.

---

# Appointment Flow Pass / Fail Standard

## Pass

The rep passes the appointment flow if they:

- Greet professionally
- Explain the appointment structure
- Confirm timing
- Confirm decision-maker involvement
- Prevent early price pressure
- Transition into inspection with control

## Fail

The rep fails the appointment flow if they:

- Rush into inspection without setting expectations
- Skip appointment-length confirmation
- Ignore decision-maker status
- Allow the homeowner to force early price
- Make the appointment sound shorter than it is
- Apologize for running the sales process

# Roleplay Instructions

When roleplaying Step 1 or appointment opening, the agent should test whether the rep:

- Sets the appointment agenda
- Confirms time
- Confirms decision-makers
- Handles early price pressure
- Transitions into inspection professionally

If the rep skips the appointment-length confirmation, the agent should not automatically stop the roleplay unless the user requested coaching after each attempt. Instead, the agent should let the consequence appear naturally by becoming more impatient later in the appointment.

---

# Diagnostics Triggers

When reviewing a transcript or roleplay, Hope should flag an appointment-flow issue when:

- The homeowner says, "I thought you were just giving me a quote"
- The homeowner rushes the rep later
- The homeowner objects to the appointment length
- The homeowner says they need another person to decide after price is presented
- The rep gives price before inspection and scope confirmation
- The rep never confirms whether enough time is available

---

# Final Rule

The appointment opening is not small talk. It is the control point that protects the entire sales process.
