# Warranty Claim Limits and Intake Process

## Purpose

This document explains warranty claim limits and the simple customer-intake process for warranty concerns.

The goal is to help reps and the AI coach respond clearly without overpromising coverage.

The signed contract and written warranty document are always the final authority.

---

# Coaching Philosophy

Warranty coaching should be simple and low-interruption.

Do not interrupt reps for minor wording issues.

Correct immediately only when the rep creates a clear overpromise or invents terms.

---

# Big Exclusions

Mention these when relevant:

- Storms
- Acts of God
- Misuse
- Alterations
- Cleaning
- Caulking
- Normal wear and tear
- Anything not on the original contract
- Pre-existing structural issues
- Rot, framing, or foundation issues not included in the original scope
- Damage from third-party work after install
- Cosmetic issues outside written warranty terms

---

# What Is Typically Not Covered

Warranty coverage generally does not cover:

1. Damage from acts of nature beyond rated wind or impact specifications.
2. Damage caused by owner modification.
3. Pre-existing structural problems.
4. Rot, framing, or foundation issues not included in the original contracted scope.
5. Cosmetic issues outside written warranty terms.
6. Damage caused by third-party work after installation.
7. Cleaning, caulking, maintenance, misuse, or normal wear and tear unless specifically included in writing.

---

# Safe Customer Explanation

Use this when explaining exclusions:

> No warranty covers everything. We stand behind our installation and help with manufacturer issues we can. Things like storms, normal wear, cleaning, caulking, third-party work, and anything outside the original contract are not covered unless the written warranty says otherwise.

---

# Warranty Intake Process

When a customer calls with a warranty concern, follow this simple intake process:

1. Ask which product the issue is about.
2. Ask when it was installed.
3. Ask what exactly is happening.
4. Ask whether it looks like installation, leak, peeling, product defect, or damage.
5. Request photos and a short written description.
6. Explain that SuperiorPRO will review the issue using the contract and written warranty document.
7. Do not promise coverage before review.

---

# What Not To Say

Do not say:

- "That is definitely covered."
- "We will fix it for free."
- "The manufacturer will approve that."
- "Everything is covered."
- "You do not need your paperwork."
- "We will take care of it no matter what."
- "That is not our problem" in a dismissive way.

---

# Approved Response When Unsure

> I do not want to guess on warranty coverage. SuperiorPRO will review the contract, warranty document, photos, and issue details before confirming what applies.

---

# Hope Coaching Rules

## Soft Correction

Use when the rep is directionally right but needs cleaner language.

Example:

```text
Good direction. Just avoid promising coverage before review. Say: "SuperiorPRO will review the contract, warranty document, photos, and issue details before confirming what applies."
```

## Hard Correction

Use when the rep clearly overpromises.

Example:

```text
Pause. That overpromises warranty coverage.
Issue: You said it is definitely covered before review.
Corrected language: "SuperiorPRO will review the issue using the contract and written warranty document before confirming what applies."
```
